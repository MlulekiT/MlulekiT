<p align="justify">
I am a population scientist whose research examines how <strong>demographic change, household contexts and social inequalities shape population health</strong> across the life course in <strong>South Africa and Sub-Saharan Africa</strong>.
</p>

<p align="justify">
My research examines population processes and the social contexts within which they occur, from <strong>fertility, mortality and migration</strong> to <strong>ageing, households, disability, health inequalities and healthy longevity</strong>. My work sits at the intersection of population studies, demography and public health, using population-based data to understand health and wellbeing within changing social and demographic contexts.
</p>

> **Population health across the life course in Sub-Saharan Africa.**

---

## 🔬 Research interests

- **Population dynamics:** fertility, mortality, migration, ageing, urbanisation, population structure and demographic change.
- **Population health:** health and wellbeing across the life course.
- **Health inequalities:** social and demographic inequalities in health and wellbeing.
- **Households and families:** household structure, kinship and intergenerational relations.
- **Ageing and longevity:** older adults, living arrangements, disability and healthy ageing.
- **Maternal and reproductive health:** reproductive health and early-life population health.
- **Population and development:** demographic change and its implications for planning, policy and development.

## 🌍 Current research direction

<p align="justify">
My research is increasingly organised around a broader <strong>life-course population-health framework</strong>:
</p>

**Population change → households and social context → health across the life course → inequalities → population wellbeing**

Current and emerging work includes:

- Changing household and family structures.
- Ageing within households and intergenerational relations.
- Disability, health and healthy longevity.
- Population and health inequalities.
- Maternal, reproductive and early-life outcomes.
- Demographic change in South Africa and Sub-Saharan Africa.
- Population-based evidence for research, planning and policy.

<p align="justify">
This framework connects demographic processes with the household and social environments in which people live, age, and experience health and wellbeing.
</p>

## 📌 Research philosophy

I am interested in research that is:

- **Population-based:** grounded in demographic realities and population processes.
- **Evidence-driven:** using high-quality population and household data.
- **Contextual:** attentive to households, communities and social structures.
- **Methodologically rigorous:** transparent about measurement, inference and limitations.
- **Policy-relevant:** connecting demographic evidence to planning and population wellbeing.
- **African-centred:** contributing to knowledge on population change and health in Sub-Saharan Africa.
